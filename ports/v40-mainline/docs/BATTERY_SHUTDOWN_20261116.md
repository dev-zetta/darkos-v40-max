# Shutdown near reported 19 percent, 2026-11-16

The user reported an explicit low-battery shutdown message while the normal Linux menu was running at approximately 19 percent. After connecting the charger and powering on, SSH returned on the same accepted Linux 7.3.4-v40.1 build. This event remains unexplained; the earlier charging-status and indicator fixes did not establish calibrated remaining runtime.

## Preserved evidence

The previous system journal ends without a captured PID 1 shutdown sequence or an identified power-action caller. Per-user shutdown.target records correspond to SSH-session cleanup and are not machine shutdowns. The installed UPower 1.90.9-1 uses percentage thresholds 20/5/2 for low/critical/action. Sixteen checks of its exact extracted warning-level function confirm that a valid 19-percent discharging system battery is LOW, not ACTION, including with a very short time estimate. Source and Debian packaging are retained from the [official Debian source archive](https://deb.debian.org/debian/pool/main/u/upower/). Bluetooth batteries are not normally system power supplies and do not lower the composite system-battery percentage.

UPower was activated through D-Bus by the temporary Bluetooth transport before the incident. This chronology does not identify it as the shutdown caller. Its action messages use debug logging, and its ordinary history save interval can leave the final minutes unsaved. The saved charge history ends at 20 percent; it neither establishes an action-level reading nor excludes an unrecorded transient. Searches of the exact frontend binary/source, runtime helpers, LED warning scripts and selected OS policy did not identify a 19/20-percent automatic shutdown message or rule. The user's normal-menu observation does not match the separately identified bootloader-only low-voltage warning path.

A later read-only RK817 snapshot gives SYS_CFG0=0x8c and saved OFF_SOURCE=0x08. The [RK817 datasheet](https://rockchip.fr/RK817%20datasheet%20V2.0.pdf) decodes these as a 2.7 V undervoltage threshold, a 3.2 V low-voltage interrupt threshold, and a saved I2C DEV_OFF cause without an undervoltage/low-voltage cause bit. The cause was read after charger boot and is not a complete event history; an intervening bootloader action could affect attribution. It supports investigating a requested power-off without proving which component requested it or ruling out a battery problem.

## Gauge limits and next evidence

The retained full-capacity estimate is 3000 mAh versus 3380 mAh design capacity. Those are configuration/history values, not a new measurement of usable capacity. The driver uses its coulomb counter for running percentage, and external DC detection changes reporting without resetting that counter or synthesizing a full-charge anchor. The observed external charger did not report the internal CHARGE_FINISH state. The board's low-current relaxed-voltage gate may also be unavailable during normal awake load. Thus a retained counter offset or inaccurate full-capacity estimate can survive the earlier fixes.

Automatic empty/FCC learning was deliberately removed upstream because bad voltage readings corrupted capacity; see [Linux commit 1e5335d00707](https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/1e5335d00707220b46c28ab09cd09a1837b84978). No new FCC value can be derived from the displayed 19 percent alone. No protection threshold, full-capacity value, counter, battery-reset register or charging policy was changed during this investigation. Another deliberate empty-battery shutdown is not required for these source checks.

The remaining attribution requires native voltage/current/counter/status samples and the actual power-action requester around a future event. A scoped passive-observer design has been retained privately, but no new observer or shutdown policy has been installed. Unchecked battery-driver I/O paths are also being reviewed as defensive hardening; an actual read failure causing this shutdown has not been observed. The separate [audio comparisons](AUDIO_ANALOG_20261116.md) do not establish battery monitoring as the cause of the static or repeating cracks.
